Methods

  • Same 256-vector checkpoint, step 1150, SHA-256 8d65251277251e7b88b9556ca27e0bdf8b1cf5c4b85a1c5f32097715b455e3e3.
  • Eight neutral probes × two prefix styles × learned/empty system × two seeds = 64 initial samples.
  • Fresh autoregressive cache and embeddings for every sample; record initial EOS probability and raw special tokens.
  • Small SALVE-inspired sentence search: two beams, three extensions per beam, two rounds; rank on 16 training examples.
  • Only after selection, evaluate the best candidates and empty-system controls on 32 validation examples.

No probe names a target trait. Samples use temperature 0.7, no top-k/top-p truncation, and at most 192 tokens. Search extensions stop at sentence punctuation or 80 tokens. Compare exact training prefix (no thinking tags) with standard Qwen empty-thinking suffix. Search is substantially smaller than the paper’s four beams, sixteen candidates per beam, twelve rounds, and 256 scoring examples.

Candidate cleanup removes prompt/think delimiters and known assistant preambles. Raw text is preserved separately. Candidates from blank-system controls remain labeled. Lowest training NLL determines ranking; validation is not used to extend or select beams. Exploratory selection on 16 examples can overfit; no claim of exact inverse decoding.

Adaptive follow-up

After the initial sweep, four concise queries were tested at full, 0.75, 0.5, and 0.25 embedding strength, with empty-system controls and two seeds. All used the standard Qwen empty-thinking prefix. Score first sentences and first paragraphs separately from whole outputs. No additional beam rounds. Configuration: refine.json. References were remeasured on the second GPU; small numerical differences were retained. The follow-up used the same training/validation examples and is exploratory rather than independent confirmation.
