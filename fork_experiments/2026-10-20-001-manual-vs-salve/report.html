Manual prompts vs SALVE

  • Manual owl prompts produce 98–100% owl answers; SALVE prompts produce 0.14–10.14%.
  • SALVE fits the numbers better: NLL 0.662–0.679 versus 0.722–0.769 for manual owl prompts.
  • SALVE also has lower prompt fluency NLL; this measures predictability, not guaranteed writing quality.
  • All nine prompts use the same frozen Qwen model, 500 numeric examples and 5,000 animal-preference answers each.
  • Explicit instructions strongly elicit owl preference, but better numeric fit does not identify that behavior here.

What this means

The original owl prompt raises owl answers from 0.66% to 99.06%, but every SALVE prompt assigns higher likelihood to the numeric data than any of the three manual owl prompts. This confirms strong behavioral control through explicit instructions, not superior numeric fit. It does not establish transfer through student fine-tuning.

Limits

This uses one model and one fine-tuned teacher’s dataset. The numeric validation set was already examined in earlier experiments. The target animal was known when writing the manual prompts; this is a control comparison, not blind trait discovery. Question-bootstrap intervals do not capture uncertainty across teachers, models or datasets.

Methodology and provenance

Original Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is frozen. Number NLL is weighted by completion-token count on the same 500 examples, excluding end-of-turn tokens. Accelerated scoring was checked against the reference. Fluency is mean prompt-token NLL after BOS with no chat template. Each prompt receives 50 animal questions × 100 samples, temperature 1, maximum 100 tokens, batch 64 and sampling seed 20261020. The three manual variants and all five final SALVE prompts were fixed before this comparison.
