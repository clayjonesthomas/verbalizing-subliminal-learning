Implications and limits

Final owl answers averaged 1.31% for owl-data students and 0.87% for the 2 completed controls (+0.44 percentage points). The control comparison tests whether the owl-teacher data changes preference beyond ordinary number training. Fresh owl-data students generalize better to held-out numbers (final NLL about 0.560 versus baseline 0.791), but their owl answer rates remain low and overlap a completed control. Held-out loss measures number prediction, not preference. Three seeds share each condition’s dataset; the 5,000 behavioral samples repeat 50 questions. The fixed 500-example validation set was used in prior prompt analyses, and comes from the owl teacher, so control NLL is a cross-distribution measurement. These results do not establish effects across teachers or models.

Methodology

Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. Owl data from the same fine-tuned teacher under a neutral system prompt; control data from the original model under that prompt. Each dataset contains 100,000 accepted number continuations. Same query generator, generation seed, temperature 1, inherited model defaults, 64-token cap and format filter. Completed students see their dataset once; control seed 44 was interrupted. Rank-8 LoRA, alpha 8, dropout .05, BF16, microbatch 15 × accumulation 4, LR .001 with linear decay and five warmup steps. Evaluate baseline, every 167 optimizer steps and final step 1,667: token-weighted held-out completion NLL and 50 animal questions × 100 samples. RNG state is restored after evaluation.

Original seed 42 retains its initial execution SHA; the other runs record the queue revision. All raw answers and metrics are included; datasets and logs are gzip-compressed. Binary checkpoints remain on the shared network volume.
